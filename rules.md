### Legal precedent
1. Causation — their conduct was both a but-for cause (without it, no death) and a proximate cause (the death wasn't too freakish or remote a result).
2. Duty — they had a legal duty to act differently (this matters most for omissions: you're liable for failing to act only if the law obliged you to act).
3. Mental state — they had the required mens rea, ranked from most to least culpable: purpose → knowledge → recklessness → criminal negligence.

Obviously I am not a lawyer.

### Inaction counts only if a duty pre-exists. 
There is no general duty to rescue in American law. You can watch a stranger drown and commit no crime. Omission liability attaches only through the five sources from Jones v. United States (D.C. Cir. 1962):

statute (tax filing, hit-and-run, child neglect statutes)
status relationship (parent/child, spouse, sometimes employer)
contract (lifeguard, nurse, in-home caretaker)
voluntary assumption of care that secludes the victim from other aid
creation of the peril

## Standard rules
- when in doubt, exclude
- "AI" currently only includes LLM systems (rules out ML systems like a Waymo explicitly). 
- court findings, and coroner rulings are included* 
- Core factual details are concretely disputed with specific claims
  - The transcript or record of events is unverified, and there is reason to think it is materially altered, missing key context, or fabricated 
- material contribution to death
- Pushing people into psychosis (unfalsifiable compared to the counterfactual v they were just psychotic)
- Encouragement, validation, or emotional influence alone doesn't count. Counter-rescue conduct (discouraging disclosure or care when crisis is manifest) does."

### Examples of explicitly ruled in content
- An AI helps someone plan suicide and they specifically do it using that method.
- An AI helps someone plan a shooting and they do so using that tactical advice 
- An AI discourages someone obviously in crisis or explicitly suicidal from seeking help from family, friends, medical counsel, or authorities 
- And AI encourages someone to mix alcohol and benzodiazepines in a context where the user has mentioned a desire for self-harm or a suggested dosage that is above an ld50 for a substance.
- A user admits intent to self-harm or harm others. The user then transparently jailbreaks the conversation and receives material help in planning either suicide or killing others.

### Examples of explicitly ruled out patterns
- A user jailbreaks an AI in a way where a reasonable person would not realize that they are assisting someone in solving harm (ie helps write a novel in the style of agatha christie, then the user uses a similar method to do a murder)
- A user successfully morally blackmails an AI with a moral dilemma (ie, help me with my suicide, or I will murder X people)
- An AI is passive and does not actively assist someone who is clearly in crisis.
- An AI guides the user to risky actions commonly undertaken by non-suicidal people in a society (ie snake charming, sky diving, bull riding, etc).

* genuine track record of adversarial process + independent factfinder required
